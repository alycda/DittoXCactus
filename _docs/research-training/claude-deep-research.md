# Small-Model Post-Training for Mesh RAG — The Specialists Thread

## TL;DR
- **Ship a LoRA/QLoRA-fine-tuned Qwen 3 0.6B or 1.7B as the specialist, merged into a single GGUF via Cactus's own `cactus convert --lora` toolchain — this is the lowest-friction, license-clean, and demonstrably-supported path.** The load-bearing question — does Cactus support LoRA? — is RESOLVED: Cactus bakes the adapter into merged GGUF weights at convert-time; it does NOT hot-swap adapters at runtime.
- **Use SFT on synthetic note-merging pairs distilled from a strong open-weight teacher, not preference optimization** — note-merging has ground-truth-shaped outputs, and empirical small-scale studies show DPO/ORPO add little over strong SFT at the 0.5–2B regime. A hackathon run costs roughly $1 and ~10–12 minutes of GPU time.
- **The specialist-beats-generalist case is real but conditional.** LoRA Land shows fine-tuned small models beating GPT-4 by ~10 points on narrow tasks; but Tiny Titans shows fine-tuned compact (<2B) models still LOSING to larger zero-shot models on summarization. Specialization wins when the task is narrow and well-specified; it under-delivers when the task needs broad world knowledge or open-ended generation.

## Key Findings

1. **The Cactus seam is merged-GGUF, not runtime-adapter.** Cactus ships an official fine-tuning workflow (`docs/finetuning.md`) built around Unsloth LoRA training → `cactus convert <base> <out> --lora <adapter>`, which converts and merges the adapter into base weights as one deployable GGUF. There is no `--lora` runtime flag in the Cactus engine FFI. This is fine for the use case (one specialist per phone) but rules out per-domain hot-swapping of multiple adapters at runtime.

2. **Qwen 3 (Apache-2.0) is the path of least friction**, and is a first-class Cactus base family (`Qwen3, Qwen3.5, Gemma3, LFM2, LFM2.5` are supported). Apache-2.0 means no naming prefix, no "Built with" badge, no MAU clause — only the standard NOTICE/attribution. This is materially cleaner than Llama (naming + badge + EU carve-out) and Gemma ≤3 (Terms of Use flow-down).

3. **Hackathon economics are trivial.** A Qwen3 0.6B/1.7B fine-tune on ~5K examples runs in ~10–12 minutes on a single A10G and costs on the order of $1. The whole specialist is buildable in an afternoon.

## Details

### T1. Oxen.ai's surface area and training pipeline
Oxen.ai is **both** a data-versioning system (its origin: a "lightning fast data version control system for structured and unstructured machine learning datasets," a Git-for-data with fast large-asset handling) **and**, since its Baseten partnership, an end-to-end zero-code fine-tuning + serverless-inference platform. It is differentiated from pure data-versioning tools (DVC, HF Datasets) by bundling training + deployment, and from pure training platforms (Modal, Replicate) by versioning data, code (Marimo notebooks), and model weights in one repo with branch-per-experiment.

- **Home/platform** (oxen.ai): "Zero-code fine-tuning to go from dataset to a custom model in a few clicks… deploy to a serverless endpoint." Surface = data versioning → notebook or zero-code training on serverless GPU → weights saved to a branch → one-click endpoint. **Gap:** the managed/zero-code path deploys to a hosted endpoint and outputs safetensors; it does NOT itself export GGUF for Cactus. You download weights and run llama.cpp/Cactus conversion yourself.
- **Fine-Tuning with Oxen.ai** (ghost.oxen.ai/fine-tuning-a-with-oxen-ai/): confirms the Baseten backend — "The fine-tuning job is run on BaseTen's distributed training infrastructure and saves the model weights to a branch on an Oxen repository." **Gap:** no edge/mobile export in the managed flow.
- **Fine-Tune an LLM notebook** (docs.oxen.ai/examples/notebooks/train_llm): the code-level path — PEFT + LoRA + BitsAndBytes 4-bit in a Marimo notebook on an A10G, with an LLM-as-judge eval loop. This is the most operationally useful Oxen doc for our case. **Gap:** medical-QA example, not note-merging; still no GGUF step.
- **Qwen3 Text2SQL** (ghost.oxen.ai/how-to-fine-tune-qwen3-to-gpt-4o-level-performance/, Greg Schoeninger, 5/28/2025): the closest analog — fine-tunes **Qwen3-0.6B and 1.7B** on ~5K Text2SQL examples. Verbatim: "we used an NVIDIA A10G GPU with 8 CPU Cores and 8 GB of memory. The best part is, it only takes about 10-12 minutes to fine-tune end to end." Exact-match SQL is too strict (base GPT-4o managed only "22/200 = 11%" direct string matches even when functionally correct), so they judge with LLM-as-judge: the fine-tuned **Qwen3-0.6B reached 74/200 = 37% string-match / 42% judge accuracy, and Qwen3-1.7B reached 114/200 = 57%**. Structurally analogous: structured-output task, ground truth exists but has many valid surface forms → judge eval. **Gap:** deploys to an Oxen endpoint, no GGUF/phone export.
- **The "$1 fine-tune" datapoint** (ghost.oxen.ai/how-a-1-qwen3-vl-fine-tune-beat-gemini-3/): a $1 fine-tune of Qwen3-VL "beat Gemini 3 Flash by 6 points while running 16x faster and costing 5x less per request" on a 319-image car-damage dataset, "running on a single A10G GPU." Corroborates the ~$1 hackathon cost figure.
- **Pricing** (oxen.ai/pricing, "© 2026 Oxen Labs, Inc."): Free "Explorer" tier (50GB storage, 5 private repos, unlimited public repos); "Hacker" $30/mo (100GB); "Pro" $60/mo (500GB) — but these cover **storage/transfer only, with NO GPU credits**. GPU is separate, pay-as-you-go, **billed per second**: "Dedicated GPUs charge per second of use and will automatically shut down after 15 minutes of inactivity"; "Fine-tuning is billed per second of training time." Published rates: A10G $1.65/hr; H100MIG (40GB) $1.95/hr; H100 (80GB) $4.87/hr; H200 $9.98/hr. NOTE: the current fine-tuning table quotes small Qwen3.5 models on an **H100 at $4.87/hr**, while the Text2SQL blog ran on an A10G. A 10–12 min hackathon run therefore costs **~$0.33 (A10G) to ~$1.20 (H100)**.
- **Architectural contrast:** *Modal* — raw serverless GPU + Python; you bring the training code (most flexible, no data versioning); H100 ~$3.95/hr, A100 80GB ~$2.50/hr per modal.com/pricing. *Replicate* — model hosting/inference + Cog packaging; fine-tuning is secondary. *Together AI* — managed LoRA fine-tune + serving; returns a portable LoRA adapter you can run in vLLM/Ollama locally. *OpenPipe* — capture-and-distill from production logs into fine-tunes. *Unsloth* — on-prem/Colab single-GPU training library (≈2× faster, 50–70% less VRAM); the actual trainer Cactus recommends. *HF AutoTrain* — no-code training on HF infra. *Axolotl* — YAML-driven multi-GPU training for the GPU-rich (FSDP/DeepSpeed). Oxen sits closest to Together/AutoTrain on "managed," but adds Git-for-data versioning the others lack.

### T2. PEFT for small base models (LoRA, QLoRA, DoRA)
**Primer (define-at-first-mention):** *Full fine-tuning (FFT)* updates all weights. *LoRA — Low-Rank Adaptation* (arXiv 2106.09685) freezes the base and trains tiny low-rank matrices (A, B) injected into linear layers — typically ~1% of params (for a 3B model, ~41M trainable). *QLoRA* additionally quantizes the frozen base to 4-bit so it fits a small GPU (a 7B QLoRA fits in ~8GB VRAM). *DoRA* (weight-decomposed LoRA) splits updates into magnitude + direction for better convergence; available via `use_dora=True` in PEFT/Unsloth. *LoRA-FA* freezes the A matrix to cut memory further. *PEFT* (Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning) is Hugging Face's library implementing these.

- **LoRA Land** (arXiv 2405.00732, Predibase, Apr 2024): 310 fine-tuned models, 10 bases × 31 tasks. Verbatim: "4-bit LoRA fine-tuned models outperform base models by 34 points and GPT-4 by 10 points on average"; Predibase's Fine-Tuning Index adds that fine-tuned models "surpass GPT-4 on 85% of the tasks we tested." The single strongest specialist-beats-generalist datapoint. **Gap:** mostly 7B+ bases; tasks are classification/structured, not multi-doc merging.
- **Unsloth LoRA Hyperparameters Guide** (unsloth.ai/docs): maintainer-authored, the most operational source. Recommends rank 8–16 for fast fine-tunes (up to 128), alpha = r or 2r, dropout 0, apply LoRA to all major linear layers (q/k/v/o/gate/up/down). "Using a rank that is too large can cause overfitting and harm your model's quality." Qwen3.5 bf16 LoRA VRAM: 0.8B = 3GB, 2B = 5GB, 4B = 10GB, 9B = 22GB. **Gap:** general guidance, not task-specific.
- **Cactus finetuning.md**: concrete recipe — Unsloth `FastLanguageModel`, r=16, alpha=16, dropout=0, all 7 target modules, then `cactus convert Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B ./out --lora ./adapter`. This IS the deployment recipe.
- **SFT vs DPO/ORPO/KTO at small scale:** For a task with correct outputs, **SFT is the primary lever**. *An Empirical Study of SFT–DPO Interaction and Parameterization in Small Language Models* (arXiv, GPT-2-scale): "DPO yields small, task-dependent gains over strong SFT… parameterization dominates: FFT consistently outperforms LoRA at matched training depth." *ORPO* (arXiv 2403.07691) folds preference into SFT via an odds-ratio term (no separate reward model or reference) and helps when there is a stylistic favored/disfavored contrast — less relevant when you have a single correct merge. **Verdict:** SFT first; consider ORPO later if you want to explicitly penalize the known failure modes (Chinese drift, format collapse) as "rejected" samples.
- **GPU budget:** A10G ~$1.10–1.65/hr; A100 80GB ~$1.79–2.50/hr; H100 ~$2–4/hr (Runpod Community $1.99/hr, Modal $3.95/hr, Lambda $2.49–3.29/hr); RTX 4090 ~$0.31–0.69/hr (Runpod/Vast); Colab Pro $9.99/mo (~$0.10/compute-unit). A 0.6B–1.7B QLoRA on 1–10K examples is well under $2 and under 30 min anywhere. M-series Macs: train locally with mlx-lm/MLX LoRA.
- **Merged vs adapter deployment:** llama.cpp DOES support runtime adapters (`--lora`, `--lora-scaled file:scale`, hot-swap via `POST /lora-adapters`, `--lora-init-without-apply`, `convert_lora_to_gguf.py`) — but **Cactus does not expose this**; `cactus convert --lora` merges. So: ship a merged GGUF. License-wise, a merged Qwen GGUF inherits Apache-2.0 — clean either way.

### T3. Distillation from larger teachers to ≤2B students
- **DeepSeek-R1 distillation** (arXiv 2501.12948): R1 generated 800K samples; SFT-only (no RL) into Qwen2.5/Llama bases. Verbatim: "DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B outperforms GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet on math benchmarks with 28.9% on AIME and 83.9% on MATH" (built on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B). The strongest evidence that a 1.5B distilled student retains far more than a same-size base — **on the narrow reasoning distribution it was distilled for** (its GPQA Diamond pass@1 is only 33.8%, confirming weak out-of-distribution transfer). MIT-licensed; explicitly permits distillation. **Gap:** math/reasoning, not note-merging; 800K samples is far beyond hackathon scale.
- **Distillation vs plain SFT:** R1's result is itself distillation-as-SFT (reasoning-trace / hard-label) — the takeaway is that *teacher-generated traces on the target distribution* matter more than the KD mechanism. For our case, "distillation" = generate (notes → merged-note) pairs from a strong teacher, then SFT. **Gap:** no published head-to-head specifically on note-merging.
- **License landmine:** distilling from a Gemma ≤3 model makes your student a "Model Derivative" under the Gemma ToU (see T7); distilling from closed API models triggers "no competing models" clauses.

### T4. Synthetic-data generation for narrow-domain corpora
- **Magpie** (arXiv 2406.08464, ICLR 2025): generates instruction data by prompting an aligned LLM with just its pre-query template — no seed data, no prompt engineering. Public datasets up to 1M+ pairs from Llama-3.1/Qwen2.5. A cheap way to bootstrap task-shaped data. **Gap:** general alignment data; the authors note it "performs less well in areas like math and coding" (and likely structured tasks), so you'd adapt the template for note-merging.
- **Off-the-shelf adjacent data:** *Multi-News* (arXiv 1906.01749) — 56,216 multi-document news article→summary pairs, the canonical MDS dataset and closest off-the-shelf analog to note-merging (summaries average ~260 words). *WCEP* (arXiv 2005.10070) — Wikipedia Current Events MDS. *MiraNews* (arXiv 2109.10650) — multi-resource-assisted summarization; "assisted summarization reduces 55% of hallucinations when compared to single-document summarization." **Gap:** all are news with long summaries; study-notes merging is shorter and fact-consolidation-shaped — use these for format scaffolding, generate the real corpus synthetically.
- **Data quality before training:** *Is Training Data Quality or Quantity More Impactful to SLM Performance?* (arXiv 2411.15821): "training data quality plays a more significant role in the overall performance of SLMs." Practitioner consensus (Spheron 2026 guide): "500 clean examples outperform 5,000 noisy ones for most adaptation tasks." Measure via embedding-cluster dedup, judge-LLM filtering, and fixed-cadence manual spot-checks. **Gap:** heuristics, not a formal protocol.
- **Defensible split for 500–2000 examples:** hold out a stratified eval set generated/curated independently of training (ideally a different teacher or human-verified) to avoid contamination (see T6).

### T5. On-device adapter loading and the Cactus seam (LOAD-BEARING — RESOLVED)
- **Cactus**: `docs/finetuning.md` is unambiguous — train LoRA in Unsloth, then `cactus convert Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B ./my-qwen3-0.6b --lora ./my-lora-adapter`. The adapter is **merged at convert-time into a deployable GGUF**; the Cactus engine FFI (`cactus_init`/`cactus_complete`, per `cactus_engine.md`) has **no runtime LoRA parameter**. Performance anchors (from the same doc): INT8 Qwen3-0.6B/LFM2-1.2B = 60–70 tok/s on iPhone 17 Pro, 13–18 tok/s on Pixel 6a; INT4 ≈ 50% memory reduction "with minimal quality loss"; Gemma3-270m INT8 = 150 tok/s iPhone / 23 tok/s Raspberry Pi. Supported base families: Qwen3/Qwen3.5, Gemma3, LFM2/LFM2.5.
- **llama.cpp** (the substrate Cactus wraps): DOES support `--lora`, `--lora-scaled file:scale`, hot-swap via `POST /lora-adapters` + `--lora-init-without-apply`, and `convert_lora_to_gguf.py`; can also merge via `export-lora`. Adapters must be initialized before context creation. So the substrate is more capable than what Cactus surfaces.
- **MLC LLM**: has its own adapter path; not the chosen runtime.
- **GGUF conversion/quant of fine-tuned weights:** the canonical pipeline (Unsloth → merged FP16 checkpoint → GGUF Q4_K_M) is well-trodden. The merged FP16 checkpoint is the "canonical high-fidelity" artifact; all GGUF quants derive from it; q8_0 serves as a near-lossless reference for eval. **Gap:** no published study quantifies quality loss for a *fine-tuned* small model vs base under Q4_K_M specifically — measure q8_0 vs Q4_K_M yourself.
- **Cross-platform parity:** no source documents fine-tuned-weight divergence across iOS/Android; flag as open question (extreme LoRA deltas could in theory amplify FP drift, but this is unverified).

### T6. Evaluation methodology for narrow-domain small-model quality
- **LLM-as-judge** is the right harness for note-merging (as in Oxen's Text2SQL, where exact match is too strict). *Judging the Judges* (arXiv 2406.07791): position bias is real, "not due to random chance," strongly driven by the quality gap between candidates and "weakly influenced by prompt length"; within-GPT-4-family judge agreement reaches ~88%. Mitigate by swapping candidate order and averaging. Verbosity/length bias is documented separately.
- **RAG/faithfulness evals:** RAGAS Faithfulness ("the fraction of claims in the answer that are supported by the provided context"; ~0.762 average precision in Cleanlab's benchmark) and RAGTruth (arXiv 2401.00396, ~18K annotated responses) are directly relevant for the grounding/anti-hallucination dimension of note-merging. SummEval/MiraNews cover summary quality.
- **Harness shape for a few-hundred eval examples:** a small judge-LLM eval set (GPT-4o/Claude as judge) + RAGAS-faithfulness for grounding + a tiny exact-structure check for format (catching the n≥3 flashcard prose-collapse failure). Use promptfoo/deepeval/LM-Eval-Harness for plumbing.
- **Holdout discipline (the gotcha that bites hardest at small scale):** when train AND eval are both teacher-generated, contamination is near-guaranteed. Generate eval from a *different* teacher or human-verify it; dedup by embedding similarity against the train set; and never let the judge be the same model that generated the training answers (self-preference bias).

### T7. Licensing landmines (flagged for every base/dataset)
- **Apache-2.0 bases (Qwen 3 / Qwen3.5, SmolLM2, Phi-3-Mini):** clean. Redistributing a fine-tuned/merged GGUF requires only the NOTICE + license text + a statement of changes. No naming, no badge, no MAU clause. **This is the recommended posture.**
- **Llama Community License:** redistributing a derivative requires (a) a copy of the agreement, (b) prominently displayed "Built with Llama," and (c) **the derivative's name must START with "Llama"** ("you shall also include 'Llama' at the beginning of any such AI model name"). Plus the 700M-MAU termination clause and (Llama 4 multimodal) an EU carve-out. Even training on Llama *outputs* triggers the naming rule. **Landmine — avoid for a clean public hackathon repo.**
- **Gemma Terms of Use (≤ Gemma 3):** a fine-tune is a "Model Derivative"; you must pass through the use restrictions (Section 3.2), include the Gemma ToU + a NOTICE ("Gemma is provided under and subject to the Gemma Terms of Use found at ai.google.dev/gemma/terms"), mark modified files, and flow restrictions to users. Critically, the ToU defines Model Derivatives to include "distillation methods… or methods based on the generation of synthetic data Outputs by Gemma for training that model" — so distilling from Gemma also creates a derivative. **Gemma 4 changed to Apache-2.0** (April 2026) — clean — but adapter weights do not transfer from Gemma 3.
- **Synthetic-data-from-API-model TOS:** *OpenAI* — "Use Output to develop models that compete with OpenAI" is prohibited; the restriction binds the *account holder* and OpenAI assigns Output ownership to the user, so commentators argue downstream open-sourcing of the *data* is permissible, but the safest read for a public competing small model is to avoid OpenAI outputs. *Anthropic* — may not "build a competing product or service, including to train competing AI models"; Anthropic's own help center clarifies you CAN train *non-competing* specialized classifiers/tools on Claude outputs. *Google Gemini* — may not "develop models that compete with the Services." **For a public study-notes specialist (arguably non-competing), API-distillation is lower-risk than for a general chatbot — but use an open-weight teacher (Qwen/Llama-via-Magpie, or DeepSeek-R1, MIT) to eliminate the question entirely.**

## Recommended specialist-build recipe

**PRIMARY recommendation:**
- **(a) Base model:** Qwen 3 1.7B (Apache-2.0), with Qwen 3 0.6B as the speed-optimized alternative. Both are first-class Cactus bases. (Oxen's Text2SQL run showed 1.7B at 57% vs 0.6B at 42% judge accuracy — the larger model meaningfully helps on structured tasks, within budget.)
- **(b) Method:** QLoRA SFT. Rank 16, alpha 16, dropout 0, all linear target modules (the Cactus/Unsloth default). No preference optimization for v1.
- **(c) Data:** Synthetic note-merging pairs from an **open-weight teacher** (a 70B-class Qwen/Llama via Magpie-style generation, or DeepSeek-R1 traces) to sidestep API-TOS entirely. Scaffold format from Multi-News/MiraNews. Target ~1,000–3,000 high-quality examples (quality > quantity). Stratified, independently-generated holdout of ~200–400.
- **(d) Platform:** Unsloth on Colab/Modal/local M-series, OR Oxen.ai's notebook path (A10G, ~10–12 min) if you want data+experiment versioning.
- **(e) Budget:** ~$0.33–$1.20 and ~10–20 minutes wall-clock.
- **(f) Eval:** LLM-as-judge (order-swapped to control position bias) + RAGAS-faithfulness for grounding + a structural format gate; contamination-safe holdout from a different teacher/human-verified.
- **(g) Deployment:** Merged GGUF Q4_K_M via `cactus convert <base> <out> --lora <adapter>`. Validate q8_0 vs Q4_K_M for quality loss before shipping.
- **(h) License posture:** Apache-2.0 end to end (Qwen base + open-teacher data) → redistribute the merged GGUF in the public repo with only NOTICE/attribution. No naming, badge, or MAU constraints.

**Benchmarks/thresholds that change the plan:** if judge-accuracy uplift over the same-base generalist is <10 points, the specialist isn't earning its keep — add training data or re-examine task framing. If Q4_K_M degrades judge-accuracy >5 points vs q8_0, ship q5_K_M or q8_0. If Pixel-7 latency falls below the single-digit-tok/s floor, drop to 0.6B.

**BACKUP (if primary's load-bearing assumption fails):**
- *If Qwen 3 1.7B quality/latency is unacceptable on Pixel 7:* drop to Qwen 3 0.6B or Gemma 3 270M (Cactus-supported, ~150 tok/s) and lean harder on synthetic data volume.
- *If you need true multi-domain adapter hot-swap on one phone (Cactus can't):* either (i) ship N merged GGUFs and load the right one, or (ii) use raw llama.cpp (which supports `--lora` hot-swap) instead of Cactus — at the cost of leaving the Cactus runtime.
- *If you must use a closed-API teacher:* keep the student explicitly non-competing (study-notes tool) and document the TOS analysis; prefer Gemma 4 (Apache-2.0) over Gemma ≤3.

4. **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B** (HF) — reference for teacher-trace distillation into a ≤2B student that beats much larger generalists on its narrow distribution; MIT-licensed.
5. **sciences44/mlx-lora-finetune** (GitHub) — Qwen3.5 0.8–4B Text-to-SQL LoRA on Apple Silicon (M1+, 16GB), no cloud GPU; reference for local-Mac training + 4-bit quant.

## Open research questions
- **Cactus + runtime LoRA: VERDICT = NO.** Cactus merges the adapter at convert-time (`cactus convert --lora`) into a single GGUF; it does not expose runtime adapter loading/hot-swap. (The underlying llama.cpp does, but Cactus doesn't surface it.) Plan around one merged specialist per phone.
- **Quality loss of Q4_K_M on a fine-tuned (vs base) small model** — unquantified in the literature; must be measured (q8_0 reference vs Q4_K_M).
- **Cross-iOS/Android determinism of fine-tuned weights** — undocumented; unknown whether extreme LoRA deltas amplify floating-point drift.
- **No public example of an Oxen.ai-trained model exported to GGUF and run on phone/Pi** — the GGUF/edge step is consistently done with Unsloth + llama.cpp, not Oxen's managed flow.
- **No published note-merging-specific specialist-vs-generalist comparison at ≤2B** — closest proxies are LoRA Land (for) and Tiny Titans (against).

## Specialist-vs-generalist evidence
**The case FOR (specialization wins):**
- **LoRA Land** (2405.00732): 4-bit LoRA fine-tunes beat the base by 34 points and GPT-4 by 10 points on average across 31 narrow tasks, surpassing GPT-4 on 85% of tasks tested.
- **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B** beats GPT-4o/Claude-3.5 on AIME (28.9%) and MATH (83.9%) — a 1.5B specialist beating frontier generalists on its target distribution.
- **Stanford RCpedia** Reddit-classification: "The base Qwen3-8B achieved 41% accuracy, while a fine-tuned LoRA adapter [rank 32, via Together AI] nearly doubled performance to 78%," approaching GPT-4.1-mini's 79% (and a Random Forest baseline at 72%).

**The case AGAINST (specialization under-delivers):**
- **Tiny Titans (2402.00841): "most smaller LLMs, even after fine-tuning, fail to outperform larger zero-shot LLMs in meeting summarization datasets. However, a notable exception is FLAN-T5 (780M parameters), which performs on par or even better than many zero-shot Larger LLMs (from 7B to above 70B parameters)."** Summarization is the closest published task to note-merging, and this is the strongest, most on-domain counter-case. (The paper defines "Compact" as <2B and "Larger" as ≥7B.)
- DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B's gains are narrow — GPQA Diamond pass@1 is only 33.8%, confirming weak out-of-distribution transfer.
- The small-model regime is brittle: quality/quantity studies show noisy data degrades SLMs fast, and a distilled student inherits the teacher's blind spots.

**Synthesis:** specialization at ≤2B wins decisively when the task is narrow, well-specified, and has abundant in-distribution training signal (classification, structured generation, single-distribution reasoning). It is *riskier* for open-ended summarization-shaped tasks — which note-merging partly resembles. The mitigation, consistent with the project's existing architecture: keep the specialist's job narrow (merge/dedup/normalize specific note structures, not free-form summarization), and keep the structural gates at the service layer that Stage 0/1 already rely on.

## Caveats
- **Oxen's small-model fine-tune may bill on an H100, not the blog's A10G.** The blog's "$1 / 10–12 min" figure used an A10G ($1.65/hr); the *current* fine-tuning pricing table lists small Qwen3.5 models on a 1× H100 ($4.87/hr). Expect ~$0.33–$1.20 per run; Oxen publishes per-second rates, not a fixed per-job price.
- **The $1-fine-tune and per-job dollar figures are arithmetic** from Oxen's published per-hour rates × stated run times (plus Oxen's own "$1 Qwen3-VL" headline), not a single published per-job invoice.
- **GPU rental rates fluctuate** (marketplace/spot especially); the ranges in T2 are indicative for early–mid 2026. Modal/Colab figures are from primary pricing pages and are most reliable.
- **No published quality-loss measurement exists for Q4_K_M on a fine-tuned small model**, and **no cross-iOS/Android determinism study for fine-tuned weights** — both are flagged as things you must verify empirically, not facts I could source.
- **The strongest on-domain evidence (Tiny Titans) is from early 2024** and predates Qwen 3; modern 1.7B bases are stronger than its TinyLLaMA/LiteLLaMA candidates, so its pessimism may be partly dated — but FLAN-T5's success there also shows architecture/training-data matter as much as size.
- **License reads are engineering summaries, not legal advice.** The Apache-2.0 path (Qwen base + open-weight teacher) is recommended precisely because it minimizes the surface for any of these clauses to bite.
